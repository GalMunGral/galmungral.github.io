Some Thoughts on AI Coding
關於 AI 編程的一些思考

When I was in college, I began in civil engineering, moved through physics and math, and finally settled on computer science. It was a difficult decision—I hated the hype-driven tech culture, but I loved that it was probably the only field outside mathematics where proofs were a basic requirement, not merely a nice-to-have. (Looking at you, physics!)

讀大學的時候，我先學的是土木工程，之後轉到物理和數學，最後才定在計算機科學上。這是一個艱難的決定：我討厭那種被炒作裹挾的科技文化，但我又喜歡計算機科學，因為它大概是除了數學之外，唯一一個把證明當作基本要求而不只是錦上添花的學科。（說的就是你，物理！）

I still remember one introductory lecture where the professor pulled up that XKCD comic about purity and pointed at the empty space between physics and math. “We are here,” he said. “We are applied math.”

我至今記得一堂入門課上，教授調出了那幅講“純粹性”的 XKCD 漫畫，指著物理和數學之間那片空白說：“我們在這裡，”他說，“我們是應用數學。”

The truth is, I didn’t hate everything about tech. A software engineer from Facebook once came to teach us how to prepare for algorithm interview questions—which I’m still terrible at, though I do enjoy writing and sharing proofs for them. He said that if you actually need to type out the code for a LeetCode problem, you’re doing it wrong. What matters is the thought process, not the mechanical movements of your fingers. “You can literally teach a business major how to do that,” he said.

說實話，我並不是討厭科技行業的一切。曾經有一位來自 Facebook 的軟件工程師給我們講怎麼準備算法面試，那門功課我至今仍然很差，雖然我確實喜歡為算法題寫證明、和別人分享。他說，如果你真的需要把 LeetCode 題目的代碼敲出來，那說明你的方法不對。重要的是思考過程，而不是手指的機械動作。“這種事你甚至可以教會一個商科生，”他說。

I thought I was better than business majors until a required ethics course brought me back down to earth. Among all the philosophical concepts—categorical imperative and whatnot—that refuse to stick in my long-term memory, there was one chapter in that textbook I’ll never forget.

我一度覺得自己比商科生高明，直到一門畢業必修的倫理課把我拉回了現實。在那些怎麼都記不住的哲學概念（諸如“絕對命令”之類）當中，那本教材裡有一章，我永遠不會忘記。

It said basically this: software engineering is not a real profession. The field is immature. We have neither certification nor a licensing body. We don’t require an exam before someone is qualified. We don’t have standard practices. We face nowhere near enough consequences for the garbage we constantly produce. Imagine if the things we use in our daily lives—appliances, cars, bridges, buildings—were as brittle as the code we write. Imagine if surgeons were as sloppy as we are. Society would probably collapse.

它大致是這麼說的：軟件工程算不上一個真正的職業。這個領域還不成熟。我們沒有認證，也沒有執業資格機構。我們不需要通過考試就能上崗。我們沒有統一的行業規範。我們製造出的種種垃圾代碼，代價遠遠付得不夠。試想一下，如果我們日常生活中使用的東西：家電、汽車、橋樑、建築，都像我們寫的代碼一樣脆弱，會怎樣？試想一下，如果外科醫生都像我們一樣馬虎，會怎樣？社會大概早就崩潰了。

So, if it wasn’t yet a true profession in the sense that medicine, law, and civil engineering are, then it was our obligation to turn it into one. That realization eventually led me to Dijkstra and Lamport, and I really admired how they stuck to the formal roots of computing. At the end of the day, computers are discrete mathematical machines, no matter how much Silicon Valley would like you to believe otherwise. People hate math, and you can’t sell things people hate, can you?

好吧。如果它還不是像醫學、法律和土木工程那樣真正意義上的職業，那把它變成一個真正的職業就是我們的責任。正是這個念頭，最終讓我去讀了 Dijkstra 和 Lamport 的著作，我非常欽佩他們堅守計算機科學中那些形式化的根基。說到底，計算機是離散數學的機器，不管硅谷多想讓你相信別的說法。人們討厭數學，而你沒法把人們討厭的東西賣出去，不是嗎？

Fast forward a few years, and I was reading source code, documentation, man pages, web pages. Learning the latest framework that would be obsolete in two years, memorizing the quirks of some library that only mattered for a month. A world far removed from what I appreciated about computer science: when we proved that there must be at least three machines for a certain distributed algorithm to work, it meant you could tear down the entire universe and build another with different laws, and you would still need three. There is no escape from that necessity.

幾年之後，我每天做的事變成了讀源代碼、讀文檔、讀手冊、讀網頁。學習兩年後就會過時的最新框架，死記硬背某個只在這一個月裡有用的庫的種種怪癖。這和我當初喜愛計算機科學的理由相去甚遠：當我們證明某個分佈式算法至少需要三臺機器才能運行時，這意味著即便你把整個宇宙推倒重來，換一套完全不同的物理定律，你依然需要三臺機器。這種必然性無從逃避。

Back in reality, I couldn’t help feeling disappointed. Constant learning, yet nothing of real importance was being learned. I started missing my days as a student. Something felt very wrong, though I couldn’t quite put my finger on it. This feels like a blue-collar job, I thought. No offense to blue-collar jobs.

回到現實，我不由得感到失望。不停地學，卻沒學到什麼真正重要的東西。我開始懷念做學生的日子。有什麼地方不對勁，但我說不清究竟是哪裡。這感覺像是一份體力活，我心想。絲毫無意冒犯體力勞動者。

One day after work in Shanghai, I overheard a conversation from a nearby cubicle. “What did you do today?” someone asked. “What else? Hard labor.”

在上海下班後的某一天，我無意間聽到隔間那邊飄來一段對話。“你今天幹了什麼？”有人問。“還能幹什麼？出苦力唄。”

I had never thought of my work that way. And all of a sudden it clicked.

我從沒這樣想過我的工作。可就在那一瞬間，一切都豁然開朗了。

In China, if you go to the countryside, you can see these two- and three-story houses that people built themselves. They set the rebar, pour the concrete, and stack the bricks. Every house is a little different—they can look odd, but they do the job. An entire extended family can live in one. Most of the time, it doesn’t leak when it rains. Sometimes they put shiny porcelain tiles on the street-facing side to make it look grand, but walk around to the back and it’s just raw concrete and bricks.

在中國，如果你去鄉下，會看到許多兩三層的房子，都是村民自己蓋的。他們就那樣鋪上鋼筋，澆上混凝土，一層層把磚砌起來。每家蓋的都不太一樣，看起來有點滑稽，但確實能住人，一大家子都能住進去。下雨的時候大多數情況下不會漏。有時候他們會在朝街的一面貼上光鮮的瓷磚，顯得體面，但繞到後面一看，就是裸露的混凝土和磚塊。

I quit my job and started roaming the city. Then one day I finally realized: writing code is like being a construction worker, minus the physical danger. Construction work can be a job, but it’s never a profession. To have standards, guarantees, predictability—you need civil engineering. And you need math. Even more so when our bricks are made of discrete math, whether or not we want to admit it.

我辭掉了工作，開始在城市裡四處閒逛。有一天我終於想明白了：寫代碼就像做建築工人，只是少了點人身危險。建築可以是一份零工，但它永遠算不上一個職業。要有標準、有保證、有可預測性，你需要的是土木工程，你需要數學。既然我們手上的“磚塊”本質上是離散數學做成的，不管我們願不願意承認，這一點就更加成立。

Why math? Why do I keep bringing it up?

為什麼是數學？為什麼我一直反覆提起它？

I like what Leslie Lamport said in a lecture about thinking beyond code: it’s not the content of math that matters—analysis, topology, all of that. What matters is how it teaches you to think. I’m no mathematician, but the most important lessons math taught me were these:

我很喜歡 Leslie Lamport 在一次談“超越代碼的思考”的講座裡說的話：重要的不是數學的具體內容，什麼分析、拓撲之類的，重要的是它教會你如何思考。我算不上數學家，但數學教給我的最重要的幾條道理是這些：

First, clarify. Human language is ambiguous. People use the same words to mean different things. We constantly talk past one another. Most debates lead nowhere because we don’t even agree on the basic definitions.

第一，先釐清。人類的語言是模糊的。人們用同一個詞表達不同的意思，我們時常各說各話。大多數爭論最終無疾而終，就是因為我們連最基本的定義都沒能達成一致。

Second, justify. Treat every claim as unproven by default. Accept nothing at face value. If you’re too lazy to support a claim, at least acknowledge that it’s an assumption that could very well turn out to be wrong. Or call it an axiom if you’re full of yourself.

第二，講論證。默認把一切主張都當作未經證實的。沒有什麼可以不加論證就被接受。如果你懶得去論證一件事，那至少承認它只是一個假設，完全有可能是錯的。或者，如果你自視甚高，也可以稱它為一條公理。

Third, simplify. This is the great paradox: people are scared of math because of how foreign it feels. It looks complicated, sure, but its purpose isn’t to complicate. Quite the opposite—it simplifies the world to such an extreme degree that we can actually fit it in our tiny brains. Math makes complicated things simple, so even though it appears hard at first, it becomes clearer and easier over time. Natural language, on the other hand, may feel easy at first, but its fuzziness overcomplicates things. Over time, even simple ideas become messy and difficult, until eventually your head explodes.

第三，做簡化。這是最大的悖論：人們害怕數學，是因為它顯得如此陌生。它看上去很複雜，沒錯，但它的目的從來不是讓事情變複雜。恰恰相反，它把這個世界簡化到了極致，簡化到我們那顆小小的大腦真的能夠裝下它。數學把複雜的東西變簡單，所以哪怕它一開始顯得艱深，隨著時間推移會變得越來越清晰、越來越容易。而自然語言恰恰相反，一開始或許顯得輕鬆，但因為它天生模糊、容易把事情搞複雜，哪怕再簡單的想法，日子久了也會變得混亂難解，直到最後讓你頭都要炸了。

Speaking of ambiguous language—we’ve been doing exactly that with our own field. For too long we’ve been conflating code production with software engineering, when the difference between repetitive, mechanical labor and critical intellectual work—like construction versus civil engineering—couldn’t be more stark. It would be absurd to call construction workers civil engineers. Yet we call code monkeys software engineers.

說到模糊的語言，我們對自己這個行業做的正是這件事。太長時間以來，我們一直把“寫代碼”和“軟件工程”混為一談，而重複性的機械勞動與關鍵性的智力工作之間的差別，就像建築施工和土木工程之間的差別一樣，再明顯不過。把建築工人稱作土木工程師，會顯得很荒唐。可我們卻把碼農叫作軟件工程師。

I won’t apologize for the derogatory language. To have taste is to discriminate. It’s better to be offended than to miss the reality: thinking is different from doing. Thinking is what we need to turn our profession into a true one. Not to execute and implement, but to envision, plan, design, abstract, reason, critique, reflect, and most importantly, simplify. That’s the key message from Dijkstra and Lamport. What we need is intellectual command of the whole edifice, not physical control of a single brick.

我不會為這種帶貶義的說法道歉。有品味，就意味著要有所區分。寧可冒犯人，也不要錯失這個現實：思考不同於執行。思考才是把我們這個行業變成真正職業所需要的東西。不是去執行、去實現，而是去構想、去規劃、去設計、去抽象、去推理、去批判、去反思，最重要的是，去簡化。這正是 Dijkstra 和 Lamport 想傳達的核心信息。我們需要的是對整座大廈的思想掌控，而不是對某一塊磚的體力掌控。

So when I first saw tools like Cursor and Claude Code generating working implementations without me having to keep up with whatever the hottest framework or library of the day was, I thought: maybe the day has finally come. Maybe we’re shedding our chrysalis as construction workers and spreading our wings as civil engineers.

所以，當我第一次看到 Cursor 和 Claude Code 這類工具能夠生成能用的實現，而不需要我再去追趕當下最熱門的框架或庫時，我想：也許這一天終於到來了。也許我們正在褪去建築工人的蛹殼，展開土木工程師的翅膀。

I look forward to that future.

我期待那個未來的到來。
